## Slide 2 — Project overview
- Introduce the fictional tourism concept.
- Explain the opportunity: presenting travel inspiration in one clear landing page.
- State that it is a student demonstration, not a live travel company.

## Slide 3 — Target audience and objective
- Local and international travellers, nature lovers, and culture seekers.
- Primary goal: encourage visitors to explore and prepare an enquiry.
- Secondary goals: showcase destinations and example packages.

## Slide 4 — Technology and development approach
- HTML5: semantic structure and content.
- CSS3: layout, visual identity, and responsive design.
- No JavaScript: CSS-only interactions (`:target` menu and form feedback, radio-based testimonials) plus native HTML5 form validation.
- Explain why custom coding was selected and discuss its trade-offs.

## Slide 5 — Design and branding
- Explain the forest-green, sand, cream, and red-accent palette.
- Discuss typography, spacing, imagery, and visual hierarchy.
- Explain how the design evokes outdoor travel and the highlands.

## Slide 6 — Landing page sections
- Hero and primary CTA.
- Experiences and destinations.
- Example tour packages.
- About and how-it-works sections.
- Sample testimonials, social placeholders, and contact form.

## Slide 7 — Headlines and calls to action
- Headline: “Discover the Kingdom in the Sky.”
- Explain how CTAs guide visitors to experiences and the enquiry form.
- Demonstrate the package buttons linking to the enquiry form.

## Slide 8 — Graphics, reviews, and social links
- Explain the role of destination imagery.
- State that testimonials are clearly marked as fictional samples.
- Explain why social accounts are shown as unconfigured placeholders.

## Slide 9 — Responsive design and accessibility
- Demonstrate desktop and mobile layouts.
- Show keyboard focus, mobile menu, labelled form fields, and reduced-motion support.
- Discuss image alt text and readable contrast.

## Slide 10 — Testing
- Present the checks actually completed.
- Show navigation, testimonial controls, and native form validation.
- Be honest about any checks not performed.

## Slide 11 — Hosting and deployment
- Explain the selected free hosting platform.
- Show the public URL after deployment.
- Demonstrate the live page from a browser.
- Explain how future changes are published.

## Slide 12 — Assessment mapping and conclusion
- Creativity: original editorial-style layout.
- Graphics: relevant scenic imagery.
- Headlines: clear hero message.
- CTAs: functional section navigation and enquiry path.
- Requirements: semantic, responsive, accessible implementation.
- Builder choice: documented custom-code rationale.
- Social links/reviews: transparent placeholders and sample reviews.
- Hosting: show the verified live URL.
- Conclude with limitations and possible future backend integration.

## Speaking and demonstration advice
- Divide sections fairly among group members.
- Understand the code before presenting it.
- Do not claim that a demo form sends enquiries.
- Do not present fictional testimonials or prices as real.
- Only report tests and deployment steps that have actually been completed.
